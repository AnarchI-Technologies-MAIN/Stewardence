One additional Core recovery finding from the concurrency review: migration 0012 lines 142–153 can acquire multiple tenant control locks in inconsistent order.

recover_jobs orders candidates by lease expiry/job ID, then retains each tenant’s transaction-level advisory lock until commit. Overlapping recovery calls can see different candidate sets. A stale candidate can make one call acquire A before waiting for B, while another holds B before requesting A. FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED only skips job-row locks; it does not skip the preceding advisory-lock wait.

This is a source-derived deadlock scenario, not an executed failure or tenant-data escape. PostgreSQL’s deadlock handling may abort one transaction, but recovery liveness and safe retry still need qualification.

Use a consistent global order for the complete selected tenant-lock set, or make recovery transactions tenant-bounded. Preserve control-before-job ordering across the other operations.
